🧱 Bacterial Cell Walls

Why Cell Walls Matter

  • Provide shape and structural support
  • Protect from osmotic pressure
  • Allow classification into Gram-positive, Gram-negative, and outliers
  • Predict antibiotic susceptibility
  • Explain staining reactions and fever production
🔵 Definition
Peptidoglycan — A polymer unique to bacteria made of alternating NAM (N-acetylmuramic acid) and NAG (N-acetylglucosamine) sugars connected by glycosidic bonds, with peptide cross-links for strength.

Gram-Positive vs. Gram-Negative

FeatureGram-PositiveGram-Negative
Peptidoglycan layerThick (multiple layers)Thin (single layer)
Outer membraneAbsentPresent
LPSAbsentPresent
Gram stain resultPurplePink
Penicillin sensitivityGenerally more sensitiveGenerally less sensitive
🟣 High Yield
Gram-positive = thick peptidoglycan = PURPLE | Gram-negative = thin peptidoglycan + LPS = PINK
🟠 Exam Tip
Peptidoglycan is unique to bacteria — humans lack it entirely — making it an excellent antibiotic target with minimal host toxicity.

🔬 Gram Stain

A differential stain that separates bacteria based on cell wall structure. The first identification step in the microbiology lab.

The 4 Steps

1
Crystal Violet (Primary Stain)All bacteria turn purple
2
Gram's Iodine (Mordant)Forms crystal violet-iodine complex inside all cells
3
Acetone Alcohol (Decolorizer)Removes complex from Gram-negative only; Gram-positive thick wall retains it
4
Safranin (Counterstain)Gram-negative = pink; Gram-positive remain purple
🟣 High Yield
Positive control: Staphylococcus epidermidis (Gram+ cocci) | Negative control: Escherichia coli (Gram− bacilli)
🔴 Common Mistake
Over-decolorizing can make Gram-positive cells appear Gram-negative. Under-decolorizing does the opposite. The decolorization step is the most technique-dependent.

Cell Morphology Terms

  • Cocci — spherical
  • Bacilli — rod-shaped
  • Diplococci — pairs of cocci
  • Streptococci — chains of cocci
  • Staphylococci — clusters of cocci

🧫 Acid-Fast Bacteria

🔵 Definition
Mycolic acid — A waxy lipid in Mycobacterium cell walls that repels aqueous stains and makes them resistant to standard Gram staining.

Acid-Fast Stain (Ziehl-Neelsen) Steps

1
Carbol Fuchsin (Primary Stain)Heat drives pink stain into waxy wall; all cells become pink
2
Acid Alcohol (Decolorizer)Mycobacteria retain pink; all others become colorless
3
Crystal Violet (Counterstain)Non-acid-fast = purple; Acid-fast remain pink
🟣 High Yield
Acid-fast = PINK (Mycobacterium) | Non-acid-fast = PURPLE (all others)
Controls: Positive = M. smegmatis | Negative = S. epidermidis
🟢 Clinical Correlation
Acid-fast stain is performed on sputum when TB is suspected. Because M. tuberculosis takes 20+ days to grow in culture (24-hr generation time), the rapid stain result is critical for early diagnosis.

🔥 LPS & Endotoxin

Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is found only in the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria.

LPS Components

  • O Antigen — Polysaccharide chain; used to identify bacterial strains
  • Core Polysaccharide — Structural linkage region
  • Lipid A — The toxic component — this IS the endotoxin
🔵 Endotoxin vs Exotoxin
Endotoxin (Lipid A): Part of bacterial structure; released when bacteria die; triggers fever cascade.

Exotoxin: Secreted protein; actively produced by living bacteria.

Fever Cascade — The Pathway

1
Macrophage phagocytoses Gram-negative bacteriaBacteria are engulfed and destroyed by lysosomes
2
Lipid A is releasedReleased from destroyed bacterial outer membrane
3
Macrophage secretes Interleukin (IL-1)Cytokine signal sent to hypothalamus
4
Hypothalamus produces ProstaglandinTemperature set-point is raised
5
FEVERBody temperature elevates
🟣 High Yield — Memorize This Chain
LPS → Lipid A → Interleukin → Prostaglandin → Fever

💊 Cell Walls & Antibiotics

🔵 Definition
Antibiotics — Metabolic poisons that selectively target bacterial metabolism without harming the host. They may inhibit cell wall synthesis, protein synthesis, DNA synthesis, or metabolic pathways.

Penicillin — Mechanism of Action

1
Normal: PBP creates peptide cross-linksPenicillin-Binding Protein (PBP) cross-links peptidoglycan strands for wall strength
2
Penicillin binds PBPBeta-lactam ring irreversibly blocks PBP active site
3
No cross-links formCell wall becomes structurally weak
4
Bacterial deathOsmotic pressure ruptures the weakened wall

Beta-Lactamase Resistance

Some bacteria produce beta-lactamase (penicillinase) which destroys the beta-lactam ring and inactivates penicillin. Natural selection ensures resistant bacteria survive antibiotic exposure and proliferate.

🟠 Exam Tip
Gram-positive bacteria are generally MORE sensitive to penicillin than Gram-negative bacteria (Gram-negative outer membrane acts as additional barrier).
🔴 Common Mistake
Overuse of antibiotics does NOT cause resistance mutations — it SELECTS for bacteria that are already resistant via natural selection.

🫁 Mycoplasma

🟣 High Yield
Mycoplasma is the ONLY major bacteria with NO cell wall. It is pleomorphic (variable shape), an obligate parasite, and the smallest self-replicating bacterium.

Mycoplasma pneumoniae

Causes Primary Atypical Pneumonia ("Walking Pneumonia")

Pathogenesis Steps

1
Attaches to ciliated respiratory epitheliumSpecial attachment proteins bind to airway cells
2
Destroys cilia and mucociliary escalatorMechanical clearance of pathogens is lost
3
No bloodstream invasionRemains localized to respiratory tract
4
Dry, nonproductive coughResult of cilia destruction; mucus not cleared

Atypical Pneumonia Symptoms

  • Low-grade fever
  • Dry, nonproductive cough
  • Hoarseness and wheezing
  • Diffuse infiltrates on chest X-ray (looks worse than patient feels)
  • Cultures require up to 2 weeks — diagnosis is largely clinical
🟢 Clinical Correlation
Because Mycoplasma lacks a cell wall, it is RESISTANT to penicillin and all other beta-lactam antibiotics. Treatment requires erythromycin or tetracycline.
🟠 Exam Tip
Mycoplasma cannot survive independently outside a host due to lack of cell wall — it is an obligate parasite.

🫀 Tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis)

Clinical Symptoms

  • Cough
  • Fever
  • Weight loss
  • Night sweats
  • Hemoptysis (bloody sputum)
🟣 High Yield
Infectious dose: ~10 bacteria | One cough: ~3000 infectious droplets | Generation time: 24 hours | Visible colonies: 20+ days

Pathogenesis

1
Inhaled into alveoli
2
Macrophages phagocytose bacteria
3
Mycobacteria PREVENT lysosome fusionBacteria survive inside macrophages
4
Immune system forms a TUBERCLESurrounds infected macrophages with collagen wall
5
Caseous necrosisCenter of tubercle dies → cheese-like consistency
6
If tubercle ruptures: reactivation TBDormant bacteria re-emerge → secondary tuberculosis

Treatment (RIPE Regimen)

DrugMechanismEffect
RifampinInhibits RNA polymeraseBactericidal
IsoniazidInhibits mycolic acid synthesisStatic or Cidal
PyrazinamideDisrupts membrane transportActive intracellular
EthambutolInhibits mycolic acid synthesisBacteriostatic
🟢 Drug Resistance
MDR-TB: Resistant to 2 first-line drugs | XDR-TB: Resistant to additional second-line drugs | Treatment may last up to 2 years.

🧪 Bacterial Culture & Growth

Culture Media Types

  • Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA/TSY) — General purpose
  • Blood Agar — Enriched; detects hemolysis
  • Chocolate Agar — Lysed blood; for fastidious organisms
  • MacConkey Agar — Selective for Gram-negative; detects lactose fermentation

Binary Fission

  • Bacteria reproduce by binary fission
  • Most medically important bacteria divide every 20–30 minutes
  • A visible colony ≈ 1 million bacteria
  • Each colony originates from ONE bacterial cell → all cells genetically identical

Lab Identification Steps (In Order)

1
Colony morphologyShape, size, texture, pigmentation
2
Gram stainFirst identification step — staining reaction + morphology
3
Cell morphologyShape under microscope
4
Metabolic testingBiochemical reactions
5
AntibiogramAntibiotic susceptibility pattern
🟠 Exam Tip
The Gram stain is ALWAYS the first identification step after colony observation. It provides two key pieces of info simultaneously: staining reaction (+ or −) AND cell morphology (shape).

⚡ Rapid Review

⭐ High-Yield Facts

  • Gram+ = thick peptidoglycan = PURPLE
  • Gram− = thin PG + LPS = PINK
  • Acid-fast = pink; non-acid-fast = purple
  • Lipid A = endotoxin = fever
  • Mycoplasma = NO cell wall = pleomorphic
  • PBP makes peptide cross-links
  • Beta-lactamase destroys penicillin
  • Mycobacteria survive INSIDE macrophages
  • Tubercle = macrophages + bacteria + collagen
  • Caseous necrosis can reactivate TB

🔵 Fever Pathway

  • LPS → Lipid A released
  • Macrophage detects Lipid A
  • Secretes Interleukin (IL-1)
  • IL-1 → Hypothalamus
  • Hypothalamus makes Prostaglandin
  • Prostaglandin resets temperature
  • Result: FEVER

🏥 Clinical Correlations

  • M. pneumoniae = "walking pneumonia"
  • Dry cough = cilia destroyed by Mycoplasma
  • CXR worse than patient symptoms
  • TB Rx: RIPE → RI for months
  • MDR-TB: resistant to 2 first-line drugs
  • Mycoplasma Rx: erythromycin or tetracycline
  • TB infectious dose: only 10 bacteria
  • One TB cough = ~3000 droplets

🧪 Stain Controls

  • Gram stain (+): S. epidermidis (G+ cocci)
  • Gram stain (−): E. coli (G− bacilli)
  • Acid-fast (+): M. smegmatis
  • Acid-fast (−): S. epidermidis
  • Gram stain = 4 steps: CV → Iodine → Alcohol → Safranin
  • Acid-fast = 3 steps: Carbol fuchsin → Acid alcohol → Crystal violet

💊 Drug Mechanisms

  • Penicillin → inhibits PBP → no cross-links
  • Rifampin → inhibits RNA polymerase (bactericidal)
  • Isoniazid → inhibits mycolic acid synthesis
  • Ethambutol → inhibits mycolic acid synthesis (static)
  • Pyrazinamide → disrupts membrane transport (intracellular)
  • Erythromycin/Tetracycline → Mycoplasma Rx

🔬 Cell Wall Outliers

  • Mycobacterium: has mycolic acid (waxy); acid-fast; Gram stain won't work
  • Mycoplasma: NO cell wall; obligate parasite; pleomorphic; smallest self-replicating bacteria
  • Both resist penicillin (different reasons)
  • Mycobacterium: 24-hr generation time
  • Mycoplasma: cannot survive outside host
